# Phrase Review

Phrase matches are prompts to read the sentence, not automatic deletions. A phrase becomes a useful editing target when it repeats, adds no information, or appears in a cluster of generic language.

## Usually cut when empty

- `Here is the thing`
- `It is worth noting`
- `At its core`
- `In today's ... landscape`
- `The reality is`
- `Let that sink in`
- `Make no mistake`
- `This matters because`
- `The implications are significant`
- `The stakes are high`
- `In this section, we will`
- `The rest of this article explains`

Keep one when it performs real discourse work, quotes a source, or matches the writer's established voice.

## Stock vocabulary

Review clusters containing words such as `delve`, `tapestry`, `intricate`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `robust`, `landscape`, `underscore`, `foster`, `showcase`, `testament`, and sentence-initial `Additionally`.

Ask two questions:

1. Does the word name something precise in this field?
2. Would a simpler word preserve the meaning and voice?

Replace only when the second answer is yes. Technical uses such as a robust estimator are not slop.

## Business shorthand

Translate vague business language into the actual action:

| Vague | Possible replacement |
|---|---|
| navigate challenges | name the obstacle and response |
| lean into | accept, choose, or invest in |
| deep dive | analysis, test, or review |
| moving forward | give the date or next step |
| circle back | return, reply, or meet again |
| align stakeholders | name who must agree on what |

## Do not ban parts of speech

Adverbs, passive constructions, first-person pronouns, contractions, and transitions all have legitimate uses. Edit them when they obscure responsibility, repeat a point, or weaken a verb. Do not remove them to increase a detector score.
