# English anti-slop patterns

Use this catalogue as a contextual scan. A single common phrase is rarely a problem. Repetition, clustering, and empty use are the stronger signals.

## Openers and throat-clearing

Cut or replace when they delay the claim:

- "Here's the thing", "Here's why", "Here's what matters", "The truth is"
- "Let me be clear", "I'll be honest", "Make no mistake"
- "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era where", "In a world where"
- "When it comes to", "At its core", "At the end of the day"
- "It's important to note", "It's worth noting", "Needless to say"
- "Let's dive in", "Let's unpack this", "Let's explore"
- "To understand X, we must first..." when the preamble is unnecessary

Fix: start with the claim, event, number, person, or decision.

## Mechanical contrast

Strong signals when repeated:

- "It's not X, it's Y"
- "This isn't about X. It's about Y"
- "Not because X. Because Y"
- "X isn't the problem. Y is"
- "Not only X, but also Y"
- "It stops being X and starts being Y"
- "No X. No Y. Just Z"
- "It wasn't X. It wasn't Y. It was Z"
- "Less X, more Y" used as a slogan rather than a real comparison

Fix: state Y directly. Keep both sides only when the distinction carries factual information.

## Reveal and engagement templates

- "The catch?", "The kicker?", "The best part?", "Plot twist:"
- "What happened next?", "The result?", "The answer might surprise you"
- "Read that again", "Let that sink in", "Think about it"
- "And that's okay", "You are not alone", "No shame in that" without a need for reassurance
- "This one hits different", "You won't want to miss this", "Thank me later"
- "Save this", "Bookmark this", "Agree?" as generic closers
- "Hot take", "Unpopular opinion" followed by a common view

Fix: remove the cue and present the information. Keep a hook only when it fits the author's established social voice and earns attention.

## Empty authority and significance

- "Experts say", "Studies show", "Industry leaders agree" without a source
- "Independent testing confirms" without naming the test
- "marks a pivotal moment", "watershed moment", "turning point" without a measurable change
- "underscores the importance", "speaks to a broader shift", "represents a new era"
- "the future looks bright", "only time will tell", "one thing is certain"
- "the real question", "the deeper issue", "what nobody tells you"
- invented labels such as "the coordination paradox" without a definition or established source

Fix: name the source and consequence. If neither exists, cut the claim.

## Inflated and generic vocabulary

Flag by use, not substring. Technical and literal meanings may be correct.

High-signal in generic prose:

- delve, tapestry, realm, beacon, testament, nestled
- leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, empower
- robust, seamless, holistic, comprehensive, actionable
- pivotal, transformative, groundbreaking, game-changing
- landscape, ecosystem, synergy, interplay, paradigm
- vibrant, thriving, bustling, ever-evolving
- navigate challenges, unpack an idea, deep dive, lean into
- utilize, facilitate, foster, elevate, streamline
- nuanced, multifaceted, intricate, myriad, plethora

Prefer plain verbs and concrete nouns. Do not swap one inflated synonym for another.

## Padding, hedges, and steering

- "due to the fact that" -> "because"
- "in order to" -> "to"
- "has the ability to" -> "can"
- "could potentially", "may eventually", "might ultimately" -> keep one hedge
- "really", "truly", "genuinely", "actually" when they only intensify
- "Interestingly", "Importantly", "Notably", "Crucially" when they tell the reader how to react
- "This matters because" when the next sentence can state the consequence directly
- "worth reading/checking out/exploring" without a reason

Adverbs are not forbidden. Keep those that change timing, scope, degree, or manner.

## Structural and rhythm signals

- repeated rule-of-three lists;
- paragraphs with matching length and syntax;
- every paragraph ending in a punchy fragment;
- runs such as "Fast. Simple. Powerful.";
- synonym cycling to avoid repeating the clearest term;
- section scaffolding such as "Overview / Key takeaways / Conclusion" in a short text;
- bold-label bullets where every item has the same grammatical shape;
- more headings than the length requires;
- paragraph reshuffle immunity: paragraphs can move without changing the argument;
- treadmill paragraphs: the premise is restated without a new fact, claim, or consequence;
- vague participial tails: "highlighting..., showcasing..., reflecting..." added as pseudo-analysis.

Fix structure before vocabulary. Rewriting isolated words will not repair a metronomic argument.

## Agency and voice

Question these patterns:

- passive voice that hides a relevant known actor;
- "the data tells us", "the market rewards", "the conversation moved" when a person made the judgment or decision;
- generic sociology: "people tend to", "everyone knows", "nobody designed this";
- unsupported first-person experience added to sound relatable;
- sterile neutrality in an opinion piece;
- fake intimacy or praise: "Great question", "You're absolutely right";
- recap flattery that restates someone's work before answering.

Do not force active voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Do not force "you" into academic, legal, or neutral documentation.

## Formatting and leaked artifacts

Always inspect:

- repeated em dashes or double hyphens used as dramatic pivots;
- bold on every key phrase;
- decorative emoji headings;
- title case on every subheading;
- six or more generic hashtags;
- placeholders such as `[Your Name]`, `[INSERT LINK]`, `2026-XX-XX`;
- internal tokens such as `citeturn0search0`, `oaicite`, `contentReference`, `attached_file`;
- AI-tool tracking parameters such as `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, `utm_source=claude.ai`, `utm_source=perplexity.ai`, `utm_source=copilot.com`, or `referrer=grok.com`;
- chatbot closers: "I hope this helps", "Feel free to reach out", "Let me know if you need anything else";
- reasoning leaks: "Let me think step by step", "Here's my thought process".

Strip leaked artifacts mechanically. Preserve a meaningful citation by replacing its internal token with a real, verifiable reference.
